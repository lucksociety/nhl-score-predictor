# NHL model review and v4.1 — 21 November 2026

Owner request: find where the model is failing and improve it as much as possible.

## Method

4,294 games from October 2023 to 19 November 2026 were forecast walk-forward: ratings from earlier days only, the
regression refitted every 30 days on earlier games only, the result scored under the production simulator.
`python research/walkforward-nov21.py` reproduces the headline table (`walkforward-nov21.json`). Past starting goalies are
the actual starters, which a morning forecast does not always know, so live accuracy will be somewhat lower than these
figures for every version alike.

## Where v4 was failing

1. **Too few 3-goal wins, too many 4-goal wins.** Predicted 19.1% of games decided by exactly three goals and 13.9% by
   exactly four; reality was 23.7% and 10.9%. Cause: the simulator pulled the goalie for a team trailing by three about
   as readily as in a one-goal game, so empty-net goals pushed 3-goal leads to 4. This misprices ±2.5 and ±3.5 puck
   lines and high totals.
2. **One rating speed.** A single 25-game half-life cannot be both stable and current. Screening one half-life at a
   time (15, 35, 50, 80) changes nothing, which is why the 19 November screen found no gain.
3. **Strong favourites under-rated.** Sides given 70% or more won 82% (194 games). v4.1 narrows this (73.5% predicted,
   80.0% actual on 255 games) but does not remove it.
4. **The market is still sharper.** On this season's 358 games with a stored DraftKings close: v4 0.6610, close 0.6546.
   v4.1 scores 0.6584 on the same games.

| Exact-score log loss | 3.66751 | 3.66621 | 3.65830 | −0.00921 (0.00209) |

Winner log loss by season (v4.1 minus v4): 2023-24 −0.0022, 2024-25 −0.0028, 2025-26 +0.0006, 2026-27 −0.0027.
Exact-score log loss improved in all four seasons (−0.0154, −0.0053, −0.0068, −0.0099).

**Two rating speeds** (`HP['hl'] = 60`, `HP['hl_fast'] = 10`, regression fitted from 2021-22). Fitted weights on the
attack side: slow goals +0.85, fast goals −0.40, fast expected goals +0.43, fast shots +0.71. In words: a team's
long-run scoring persists, a recent run of goals beyond its chances does not, and recent shot and chance volume is the
useful part of form. The gain does not depend on the exact pair: 6+60, 8+40, 10+40, 10+80, 12+50, 15+60 and 15+100 all
gave −0.0013 to −0.0016 on winner log loss.

**Goalie pull when trailing by three** (`SIM['pull3'] = 0.4`). Fitted on 2023-25 and checked on 2025-27, where it
held (exact score −0.0081, z −2.5; totals −0.0085, z −4.5). Values from 0.25 to 0.65 all improve every season; 0.4 is
the middle of that range. `SCALE` 0.887 → 0.896 restores the mean total. After the change, wins by 4+ goals: 16.0%
predicted, 16.8% actual (v4: 19.2%).

## Tested and not adopted

Each is the change in winner log loss on the same 4,294 games; positive is worse.

- Prior-season carry 0.5 / 0.7 / 0.9: +0.0002 / +0.0006 / +0.0010.
- Starting each season from last season's rating instead of league average: +0.0001 to +0.0014 across 18 settings.
- League-average prior of 10 / 30 / 40 games: −0.0001 / +0.0001 / +0.0002.
- Opponent-adjusted ratings: −0.0001 to −0.0002, within noise.
- Goalie shrinkage 40 / 150 / 300 shots, goalie carry 0.5 / 0.9 / 1.0, goalie recency decay: all within ±0.0002.
- Venue adjustment, ridge 0.1 / 5 / 20, recency-weighted fitting: nothing reliable (ridge 20: +0.0020).
- A direct win/loss regression, alone or averaged with the simulator: +0.0002 / −0.0002.
- Rescaling win probabilities to be more confident: helped in 2024-25 (−0.0024), hurt in 2025-26 (+0.0034). Not reliable,
  and it is the calibration change the repository gates behind 500 live games.
- Other simulator settings fitted on 2023-25 (earlier pull when down one, lower empty-net rate, weaker score effects,
  stronger overtime tilt to the favourite): gains on 2023-25 did not hold on 2025-27.

## What the market knows that the model does not

On this season's 358 games the gap between the closing line and the model is mostly a standing opinion about teams,
not noise: team averages explain 56% of its variance, and a team's average gap in the first half of the sample
correlates 0.48 with its gap in the second. The market rates Toronto, Florida, Tampa Bay, Dallas and Washington higher
than the model, and Anaheim, Vancouver, Detroit, the Islanders and Seattle lower.

Learning each team's standing from earlier closing lines and moving the forecast part-way toward it improved winner
log loss by 0.003 to 0.005 on the 298 games after the first two weeks (z about 1.5). That is the largest candidate
found, and it is not adopted: one part-season is too little to trust it, and it would make the model partly a copy of
the sportsbook line. It needs past seasons' closing lines, which are not in this repository.

## Limits

- About 80 variations were tried. The two adopted changes were the ones that held across seasons and across nearby
  settings, but some selection effect remains; the live record under `v4.1-independent` is the real test.
- The skater adjustment's coefficients were fitted on top of v4. They are small and were left as they are.
- v4.1 is not shown to beat the market. On the evidence here it closes roughly a third to a half of the gap on winners.

## Also fixed: skater feed

Every game on 21 and 22 November carried "Skater adjustment unavailable: official skater game-log feed incomplete",
which withholds every bet. The saved skater data stopped at 18 November. The fetch asked the NHL stats feed for ten
days in one unpaged request and rejected any response that was not complete. It now reads one day at a time, page by
page (`skaters.fetch_recent`), with the same completeness checks. The feed could not be reached from the build
environment, so the cause is inferred from the code and the fix is confirmed by tests only until the next live run.
